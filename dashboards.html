You are not short of numbers. You are short of the answer.

The data already exists — it is in the billing software, the stock sheet and somebody’s inbox. What costs you is the distance between the data and the decision. These are the four ways that distance shows up, and the four questions in the next section are, one for one, how a screen closes them.

01

The number takes a week to assemble.

Somebody exports, somebody pastes, somebody checks. By the time the report is circulated it describes a week you can no longer do anything about.

02

Two people, two answers.

Sales says one figure, accounts says another, and both are right by their own definition. Half the meeting goes on whose spreadsheet to believe.

03

Nobody notices until it is expensive.

Stock runs out, a customer goes quiet, a margin slips. It was in the data for weeks — nothing was watching, so nobody was told.

04

You see the total, not the reason.

Sales are down nine per cent. Which branch, which product, which week? Finding out is another day of exports, so mostly it does not get found out.

What we build

Four questions one screen has to answer.

One for each gap above. We build to these four and stop — a dashboard that answers everything gets read by nobody, and a chart that changes no decision is a chart you are paying to maintain.

Right now that answer costs two phone calls, three spreadsheets and a week. We connect the systems you already use, agree what each number means, and keep the answer current on its own — daily, hourly, or the moment something crosses a line you care about.
